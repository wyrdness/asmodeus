## Overview

Asmodeus (Hebrew Ashmedai; Greek Asmodaios; Arabic Sakhr) is a major demon figure in Jewish, Christian, and Islamic demonology. His name probably derives from the Avestan compound aēšma-daēva, 'wrath-demon', suggesting a Zoroastrian origin or influence. He first appears in the deuterocanonical Book of Tobit (c. 2nd century BCE), where he kills the seven successive husbands of Sarah on their wedding nights and is finally driven off and bound in Egypt by the angel Raphael. In Talmudic literature he is the king of the shedim and a more mischievous figure, famously tricked by Solomon into helping build the Temple in Jerusalem before stealing Solomon's throne. Christian demonology of the late medieval and early modern period assigned him oversight of the deadly sin of lust (Peter Binsfeld; Malleus Maleficarum, 1486) and integrated him as a great king or duke into grimoiric demonology — most famously as Asmoday, king number 32 in the Ars Goetia of the Lesser Key of Solomon, ruling 72 legions, depicted with three heads (bull, man, ram), a serpent's tail, and a fire-breathing mouth, riding an infernal dragon. In Islamic tradition he appears as Sakhr ('rock'), the demon imprisoned in stone after his defeat by Solomon, central to Quranic exegesis of Sūrat Ṣād 38:34 and to the Tale of the City of Brass.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Most widely accepted derivation: Avestan aēšma-daēva, 'demon of wrath', though the compound is not directly attested in surviving Avestan scripture. The Hebrew Ashmedai and Greek Asmodaios derive from this stem.
- **Meaning**: 'Demon of wrath' (Avestan); rendered in later Christian demonology as the demon of lust.
- **First use**: Book of Tobit (deuterocanonical Jewish text, c. 225–175 BCE).

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Demonic prince / king of Hell** _(Authors of the Book of Tobit, Compilers of the Lesser Key of Solomon, Peter Binsfeld, Sebastien Michaelis)_

Mainstream demonological view in Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions: Asmodeus is a real spiritual entity, a fallen angel or demon-king, who can be invoked or warded against by ritual.

### Scientific

**Iranian / Zoroastrian etymological origin** _(Edvard Stave, Jewish Encyclopedia (1906), Mary Boyce)_

Comparative-religion view: Asmodeus is a Hellenistic-period adoption of the Avestan demon of wrath Aēšma-daēva, whose attributes were progressively reshaped within Jewish and Christian demonology.

**Literary and narrative figure** _(Bernard Bamberger, Edward Langton, Joseph Peterson)_

Standard scholarly view: Asmodeus is a literary character of Second Temple Jewish, rabbinic, Christian, and Islamic textual traditions, whose iconography crystallises in the early modern grimoiric tradition.

### Skeptical

**Pre-scientific personification of human passions** _(Modern critical demonology (Robert Muchembled))_

Skeptical view: Asmodeus and the goetic kings personify human vices (wrath, lust, greed) and were used to externalise moral failure in ritual and pastoral contexts.
